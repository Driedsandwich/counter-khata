Counter Khata — a credit book for the busy kirana counter

Concept redesign · Brief DS-03 · the people and amounts in the demo are fictional

Who it is for

Kirana shopkeepers and counter staff giving goods on credit (udhaar) while serving a queue, often one-handed. The brief asks for a credit entry in under 10 seconds; here that is a target judges can time on screen, not a tested result.

What goes wrong today

  • Users of popular khata (credit-book) apps report that adding a name and amount is interrupted by ads and a two-entries-a-day free limit, and that reminders became limited or paid [1].
  • After one update, "given" (red) and "received" (green) amounts all turned black [2].
  • A village shop family keeps credit only in a notebook; one buyer's balance kept growing despite repeated verbal reminders [3].
  • When UPI stays on "processing": in one case the staff could not check the owner's bank app and the customer paid twice [4]; in another the customer took the goods on credit [5].

Limits: solo designer outside India; desk research only; no interviews or user tests yet. These accounts show the problems occur, not how often.

Three decisions

  1. Amount first. A large keypad, then one of six recent customers in thumb reach, then Save. A timer runs from the first key to Save. Trade-off: anyone outside those six is found by search.
  2. Balances you can explain. Every line shows the balance after it; "Given" and "Received" use colour, a +/− sign and a word; mistakes stay visible, struck through. Trade-off: more to scan.
  3. Reminders you read first. Pick gentle, clear or firm, see exactly what the customer will read, then copy it to send from your own chat app. Trade-off: sending stays manual.

Errors and edge states

  • After every save, a 5-second Undo bar floats over the screen, and long amounts shrink to one line, so the keypad never moves under the thumb.
  • Receive via UPI: amount → QR → a large "Received ₹250" that staff can see → ledger. (In the demo you choose the result.)
  • If UPI is still processing, the goods can still go: the amount sits on the customer's khata as credit and a card above the keypad waits. Mark received clears it; UPI failed · keep on khata keeps it. Payments are simulated.

Accessibility and language

Contrast: body 15.2:1, secondary 6.3:1, buttons 8.6:1; at least 6.1:1 under simulated deuteranopia and protanopia. Targets ≥48 px (keys 72 px), visible focus, screen-reader labels, saves announced. Three text sizes without sideways page scrolling; reduce motion. Hindi labels are AI-drafted, not native-reviewed.

Design system

The accent comes from the red cloth of a trader's bahi-khata, linked to prosperity and new account books [6].

  • #8E1C17 on paper #FBF5EA; ink #2B1B15; secondary #6A574D. System fonts; type 16 / 18 / 24 / 48 px; 8 px grid.
  • Four themes (bahi red, marigold, green, black and white), each with text at 4.5:1 or more.
