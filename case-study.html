Counter Khata — a credit book for the busy kirana counter

Concept design · Brief DS-03 · the people and amounts in the demo are fictional

Live demo: driedsandwich.github.io/counter-khata · Code, checked facts and source links: github.com/Driedsandwich/counter-khata

Who it is for

Kirana shopkeepers and counter staff giving goods on credit (udhaar) while serving a queue, often one-handed. The brief sets a 10-second target for a credit entry: here a ₹250 credit takes five taps (2 · 5 · 0, Ravi, Save), and a demo timer shows the time taken.

What goes wrong today

  • Users of one khata (credit-book) app report that adding a name and amount is interrupted by ads and a two-entries-a-day free limit, and that reminders became limited or paid [1].
  • After one update, "given" (red) and "received" (green) amounts all turned black [2].
  • A village shop keeps credit only in a notebook; one buyer's balance kept growing despite verbal reminders [3].
  • With UPI stuck on "processing", one customer paid twice because staff could not check the owner's bank app [4]; another took the goods on credit [5].

Limits: research is desk-based (public reviews and posts); no interviews or user tests yet. These accounts show the problems occur, not how often.

Three decisions

  1. Amount first. The amount is known as soon as the goods are counted, so the screen opens on a large keypad; then one of six recent customers in thumb reach, then Save. Trade-off: anyone else is found by name search.
  2. Balances you can explain. Every ledger line shows the balance after it; "Given" and "Received" use a +/− sign and a word as well as colour. Trade-off: more to scan.
  3. Reminders you read first. Pick gentle, clear or firm, read what the customer will get, then copy it into your own chat app, so nothing is sent automatically. Trade-off: sending stays manual.

Errors and edge states

  • After Save, the ledger shows "+ Given ₹250" with the new balance, and a 5-second Undo bar floats over the screen without moving the keypad. Later mistakes are cancelled from the ledger and stay visible, struck through.
  • Payments: cash from the ledger; UPI as amount → QR → a "Received ₹250" that staff can see. In the demo you choose the UPI result.
  • If UPI is still processing, the goods can still go: the amount sits on the khata as credit and a card above the keypad waits. Mark received clears it; UPI failed · keep on khata keeps it. Reminders leave out a UPI that is still processing.

Accessibility and language

The brief asks for apps that first-time and older users can use: large keys (72 px) and buttons (≥48 px), plain labels, money shown with a sign and a word, text up to extra large, and nothing sent without a preview. Contrast: body 15.2:1, buttons 8.6:1, at least 6.1:1 under simulated colour blindness. Screen-reader labels; English with Hindi main labels.

Design system

The accent is the red cloth of a trader's bahi-khata, linked to prosperity and good luck [6].

  • #8E1C17 on paper #FBF5EA, ink #2B1B15; system fonts; type 16 / 18 / 24 / 48 px; 8 px grid.
  • Four themes (bahi red, marigold, green, black and white), each with text at 4.5:1 or more.
  • Components: keypad, customer chips, ledger rows (normal, cancelled), status cards (processing, failed), Undo bar.

Try it in 30 seconds

Nothing is paid or sent. Tap 2 · 5 · 0 → Ravi → Save, then open the ledger and preview a reminder. Screens: entry, ledger, reminder, receive via UPI, UPI processing, settings. On a laptop, a side panel opens each state. Every number here is checked in the repository's Verified facts table.
